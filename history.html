Origins and timeline

The Swiss Style developed in Switzerland in the 1940s–1960s and is often called the International Typographic Style. It stresses readability, grid-based layouts, and clarity.

Timeline (condensed)

  • 1940s–1950s — Post-war graphic designers in Switzerland refine grid systems and typographic rules.
  • 1950s–1960s — Influence spreads internationally, especially in corporate identity and editorial design.
  • 1970s–present — Swiss principles are standard in modern UI and branding.

Key figures

Josef Müller-Brockmann
Swiss graphic designer known for rigorous grid systems and classic poster works.
Armin Hofmann
Teacher and designer whose approach emphasized clarity and formal discipline.
Max Bill
Artist and designer who bridged concrete art and functional design.

Context and impact

The Swiss style influenced corporate identity, signage, editorial layout, and later web/UI design. Its emphasis on objective communication and modular grids remains highly relevant.
