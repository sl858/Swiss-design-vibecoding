PHILOSOPHY OF FUNCTION

The essence of Swiss design, born from functional necessity, shaped a philosophy of functional beauty and strict simplicity.

Functionality

Every element serves a purpose. Decoration is avoided in favor of utility and clear communication, ensuring the message is always prioritized.

Grid & Space

Maximizing white space to isolate elements, using a mathematical grid for position and scale. This provides a sense of visual order and balance.

Neutrality

Sans-serif typefaces (like Helvetica) and objective photography are used to minimize subjective interpretation, presenting information without artistic bias.

Core Principles of the Style

Composition and Grid

  • **Rigorous Grid Systems:** The layout is founded on a proportional, invisible grid to achieve a structured and harmonious arrangement.
  • **Asymmetric Layout:** Elements are organized according to the grid, but often in an unbalanced or asymmetrical fashion to create visual tension.
  • **White Space as a Tool:** Space is utilized as an active element in the design, helping to clarify the relationship between objects and text.

Typography and Color

  • **Sans-Serif Focus:** Reliance on neutral, legible sans-serif typefaces for all communication (e.g., Helvetica).
  • **Objective Imagery:** Preference for photography over illustration to maintain an impersonal, factual tone.
  • **Limited Color Palette:** The purposeful use of a few colors (usually black, white, and one vibrant accent color like **yellow**) for emphasis and system definition.
